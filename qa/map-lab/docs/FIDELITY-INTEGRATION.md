# Building meshes, bridge levels and mapped supports

The live area-fetch flow now includes NYC's 2014 building scene layer, 2022 rooftop water tanks and [NYSDOT ramp, roadway and bridge inventories](TRANSPORT-INVENTORIES.md). They use the same selected bounds, cancellation, source controls, merge resolution and downloads as the other sources. The NYSDOT layers retain alignment and structural references; they do not establish deck grades or individual support positions. Research-only sources and remaining omissions are listed in [the source audit](SOURCE-FIDELITY-AUDIT.md). This implementation changes Map Lab; the authored game remains separate.

Raw snapshots and source merging retain provider observations, confirmed identities, selected measurements and all unique alternatives. Source merge never estimates or extrapolates. A separate render stage derives models and records them in `render.decisions`; `merge.decisions` remains unchanged. Bridge interpolation, rigid base placement, pylon members and tank walls cannot supply measured fidelity evidence. Actual support/equipment bases are recorded in `render.attributes.placement`, including the terrain datum or disabled-terrain plane. See [the source/render contract](SOURCE-RENDER-BOUNDARY.md).

## Building selection

Current OSM/NYC building merging runs first. The 2014 adapter retrieves the intersecting native I3S hierarchy, geometry and all identity attributes rather than relying on camera-loaded ArcGIS features. It validates binary lengths, face ownership, attribute/object identities and complete acquisition before accepting a snapshot. Original layer/node JSON and geometry/attribute bytes remain in the snapshot alongside decoded records, bounds, requests and acquisition time. A source failure does not turn a partial mesh acquisition into a complete snapshot. There is no local-data runtime fallback.

The mesh then needs a reciprocal unique match to a current NYC whole-building footprint using the existing BIN/geometry gates. Placeholder IDs, ambiguous splits and conflicting valid IDs stay review records. Source merging retains both dated geometries in the confirmed identity group. Rendering selects a display representation and requires useful stepped or sloped upper surfaces, compatible relative height, no post-capture construction-year conflict and no overlapping mapped OSM parts. Roof-band aggregation uses a canonical surface order so equivalent triangle permutations do not change selection. The thresholds live in [building matching](../pipeline/building-match.js), [merge parameters](../pipeline/map-merge.js) and [render representation selection](../render/source-representations.js); triangle count alone never determines fidelity.

A selected mesh retains its native triangles, uses the current building's canonical identity and keeps inherited OSM/current attributes as provenance. Its placement rigidly rebases the mesh's lowest elevation onto the current building base or estimated terrain. This preserves relative roof geometry but is an explicitly recorded estimate, **not an EGM96-to-NAVD88 transform**. Current footprint extrusion remains a separately recorded collision proxy. The [render topology audit](../render/mesh-topology.js) checks subdivided geometric edges, orientation, vertex fans, duplicate/degenerate faces, intersections and positive volume without repairing source triangles. Overlapping component bounds require interior-volume review, including nested non-intersecting shells. Named work limits record an incomplete audit rather than claiming validity on oversized inputs. Only models passing that audit receive estimated caps on area cuts; other models remain surface displays. Neither path establishes a native roof-walking contract.

Confirmed whole-building identities can merge while independent parts remain available. Rendering replaces a whole volume only when eligible, uniquely assigned parts cover its entire footprint and base, including holes, and their maximum top retains the parent height envelope. Raised-only and vertically short assemblies keep the whole model. Accepted parts share one explicitly estimated parent-base placement; differing maximum heights remain render review records. Exact triangle subtraction detects internal holes and narrow uncovered gaps. Original longitude/latitude building rings are retained beside projected geometry. Assembly comparisons use a common affine WGS84 source plane with a local metre scale; this preserves straight source segments at different vertex densities instead of treating projection curvature as missing coverage. The decision records identify that coordinate basis. Partial, intersecting or ambiguous assemblies retain the whole volume and keep affected parts as references with review reasons. This is horizontal coverage validation, not proof of a surveyed volumetric assembly. [Part rules](../render/building-parts.js) and [regressions](../tests/building-parts-test.mjs) preserve that distinction.

Rejected/unmatched meshes remain references and exportable source observations. Disabling the mesh source re-resolves current alternatives; turning off merging leaves native meshes as references. A mesh-only fetch cannot supply the current identity/base context needed for selection. Acquisition caps live in [MESH_LIMITS](../data/nyc-building-mesh.js); dense areas may require smaller bounds. Raw exports include binaries and can be substantially larger than rendered geometry.

[The adjacent integration measurements](fidelity-integration-measurements.json) archive source usage under the recorded policy version for one Luna Park capture, including the exact input scope. Counts depend on query bounds, source edition and which boundary-crossing buildings actually emit geometry; they are not citywide usage percentages. The browser fixture separately isolates native/current selection without OSM, so its source totals are not interchangeable with the combined-source measurement.

### Rooftop tanks

The inspected Luna Park Houses 3 mesh contains stepped roof blocks but no distinct water-tank geometry. The renderer preserves the supplied triangles; it does not remove tanks. NYC's 2022 Water Tank layer is now fetched live and identifies equipment attached to that building through BIN, mapped outlines and base/top/height fields. Its records are retained in raw snapshots and exports.

The [equipment rule](../pipeline/roof-equipment.js) requires a unique enabled rendered parent with the same valid BIN, a recorded compatible building base, strong footprint coverage, center support and compatible local roof heights. The captured tank outlines partly overhang narrow roof blocks; the rule checks area coverage and support separately instead of requiring every outline vertex to sit over the roof. Footprint evidence and rule thresholds are logged. Changed parents, invalid units/height, misplaced outlines, missing base and possible equipment already represented in the native mesh remain references.

A selected tank uses its mapped footprint and measured base/top envelope, placed rigidly with its parent. The envelope is an extrusion estimate, without invented conical caps, legs or materials. Absolute heights retain NYC's declared NAVD88/feet provenance and the building-base alignment remains provisional; see [NYC capture rules](https://github.com/CityOfNewYork/nyc-planimetrics/blob/main/Capture_Rules.md#water-tank). This is a separate feature role, not another complete building to stack over the selected mesh. Tank source toggles restore attachments; a different selected building representation must independently pass the local roof check. Equipment is excluded from ground and walking support indexes.

## Source retention

All connected adapters request complete feature properties. OSM retains the full returned response and query provenance. New Socrata captures additionally retain every response page before display deduplication and the full dataset metadata response, including unused columns/domains. LION retains complete query responses and full layer metadata. Water tanks and NYSDOT inventories retain full layer metadata, fixed object-ID inventory, native ArcGIS JSON with requested true curves/Z/M, and the GeoJSON used by normalization. The 2014 source retains original node JSON and all fetched geometry/attribute bytes, including unused normals/UV/color channels. Selection never edits these snapshots.

Downloads/export keep these raw observations alongside normalized source records; disabling a provider changes selected merge inputs; rejected observations remain available to rendering. Geometry is retained before clipping. Unknown fields and rejected/duplicate observations remain available for future rules. Metadata retrieval failures are explicit, rather than fabricated metadata. Older saved snapshots are not retroactively enriched with responses that were never captured.

“Complete” refers to returned responses for the selected area and requested representation. It does not mean the whole city, every historical edition, private attachments or original vertex Z that a 2D Socrata endpoint never returned. The audit retains those acquisition/representation gaps for further adapters. More raw retention increases export size; it does not require every alternative to enter GPU memory.

## Global bridge rules

The captured Verrazzano bridge polygon contained nearby observations from different decks. Blending those into one four-neighbor surface produced the reported wave. [Bridge level resolution](../pipeline/bridge-levels.js) now detects mixed height observations before ordinary deck interpolation. The mixed polygon remains a reference; it never becomes one blended road surface.

Sustained paired observations and two explicitly ordered OSM layers sharing bridge identity can resolve separate lower/upper longitudinal grades. Exact shared OSM nodes continue a same-level path across way splits. Layer numbers establish ordering only; actual heights come from observations. Supported intervals are bounded by measured knots and maximum gap/grade constraints. Unmeasured ends, multiple claims, unnamed/unordered layers and ambiguous clusters remain references. Ordinary supported single decks retain their existing estimated interpolation.

[Connecting bridge ramps](../pipeline/bridge-ramps.js) need two compatible height anchors at exact shared OSM nodes. The chain must be unbranched; uniquely associated road observations may add constraints. Different layer tags may meet at a real junction without implying a fixed rise. Redundant equal observations remain recorded; contradictory station heights and unsupported grades prevent selection. The connecting grade is explicitly estimated. Existing [ground approaches](INFRASTRUCTURE.md#connected-road-approaches) retain their graph-based rules.

These rules contain no bridge names, coordinates or feature IDs. The captured span and synthetic ramp cases are verified. Full live interchange topology was not acquired successfully, so this does not certify every Verrazzano on-ramp or every other bridge. Sparse or ambiguous inputs remain visible gaps.

## Mapped supports

An active OSM `bridge:support` value takes precedence over generic building classification. Pylons with a mapped outline, explicit height, one containing road structure and mapped bridge-road overlap can render a coarse open frame. The [support rule](../pipeline/bridge-supports.js) reserves road ribbons and their round joins through the frame up to the estimated top beam. The footprint and tagged height are retained; member sections, beam depth and terrain base are estimates. Unresolved road elevations are not invented to make a support eligible.

Both mapped pylons in the captured Verrazzano input produce open frames instead of solid building blocks. Abutments without usable heights remain references. No evenly spaced pillars, cables, bracing or foundations are inferred. Supports do not yet have a walking/collision contract. Aerial LiDAR catalogs do not establish measured underside/support geometry.

The attributed, checksummed [fidelity fixture](../tests/fixtures/fidelity-manifest.json) preserves native responses; it is test transport only.

[fidelity-browser-test.mjs](../tests/fidelity-browser-test.mjs) exercises the real fetch/controller flow against recorded API responses, roof/source toggles, bridge rendering and a hardware GPU benchmark under Xvfb. It writes roof close-ups, bridge views and actual timer results to ignored `.tmp/map-lab/fidelity/`. See [the test guide](TESTING.md) for commands and result scope. The original intermittent large-scene Chrome crash remains unproven and is not declared fixed by these checks.

Remaining work includes other equipment/curb-cut adapters, original NYC vertex-Z role validation, OSM roof forms and unresolved part assemblies, broader OSM interchange captures and inventory association rules, unknown support positions/dimensions, verified datum transforms and bounded LiDAR reconstruction. Further native collision development is deferred; walking remains an inspection tool. Overture lineage/height conflicts and upstream duplication need their own adapter gates. The [source audit](SOURCE-FIDELITY-AUDIT.md) keeps the full dataset inventory and archived measurements.
