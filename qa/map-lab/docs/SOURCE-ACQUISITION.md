# Acquisition, source merging and deferred rendering

“Metadata only” means catalogue descriptions, field definitions or tile indexes were saved without their actual records or point payloads. It is an acquisition gap, separate from missing rendering. A source is collected when its original area payloads are retained and exposed through `plan.observations.records`; rendering support is tracked separately.

MTA station/entrance records, original NYC planimetric geometry, pedestrian-ramp observations, building-grade/floor centroids, federal/state bridge-clearance points, NYSDOT overhead-sign asset points and NYC 2017 LiDAR now participate in **select area → Fetch area → inspect sources → merge**. They use the registered-source controls, cancellation, downloads and separate rejected-acquisition archive. Their physical modeling remains deferred. Other audited candidates without adapters remain pending; see [the source audit](SOURCE-FIDELITY-AUDIT.md).

## Building grade and floor observations

[NYC Building Elevation and Subgrade (BES)](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/City-Government/Building-Elevation-and-Subgrade-BES-/bsin-59hv) is now a live-query source. It adds reported building centroids with BIN/BBL, lowest adjacent grade (`z_grade`), an **estimated** lowest actively used floor (`z_floor`), subgrade status and qualification notes. Acquisition retains original GeoJSON pages, metadata and all fields; merge keeps reference points and complete source observations without generating a terrain, floor or building model. A BIN is a candidate relationship to a footprint. BES grade and footprint ground elevation retain separate provenance and roles; neither record is deleted. The source BIN arrives in some API records as a decimal-form string such as `1000005.0`; cross-source identity normalization and datum compatibility need validation before automatic attribute selection. Some reported rows even carry vacant-lot notes, so a row alone does not prove a present building volume.

[DCP's guidance](https://s-media.nyc.gov/agencies/dcp/assets/files/pdf/data-tools/bytes/bes_readme.pdf) says these observations derive from street-level imagery and LiDAR, and `notes1` flags obstructed grades estimated by an operator. It explicitly says BES contains **no street-level elevation measurement** and is not a complete inventory of subgrade spaces. The catalog describes elevations as feet above sea level in a vertical datum it spells “NADV88”; that spelling is retained as a source statement rather than silently equating it with other NAVD88 observations. The 2022 date refers to the source footprint edition, not a proven measurement date for each grade. The [Manhattan capture](SOURCE-SIZES.md#lower-manhattan-2-km-comparison) verifies live area retrieval and export. BES improves building-base evidence but cannot locate bridge/rail columns, pier caps or highway-ramp grades.

## Federal bridge observations and clearances

The [USDOT 2023 National Bridge Inventory service](https://geo.dot.gov/server/rest/services/Hosted/National_Bridge_Inventory/FeatureServer/0) is now an area-query source. The [live Manhattan sample](SOURCE-SIZES.md#lower-manhattan-2-km-comparison) retained 11 complete bridge records, their original ArcGIS/native and GeoJSON replies, all 128 fields and layer metadata. Reported span counts, maximum span, deck width, relative clearances, inspection condition and navigation pier-protection codes are separate evidence alongside NYSDOT bridge points. A federal structure ID is not silently equated with a state bridge BIN. The points do not locate columns or certify an absolute road/deck elevation. The federal [NBI guidance](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/bridge/nbi/ascii.cfm) cautions against route-clearance use because inventory clearance may be outdated. Field codes, units and missing-value sentinels stay raw pending dictionary-specific interpretation.

The [NYSDOT Bridge With Roadway Inventory table](https://gis.dot.ny.gov/hostingny/rest/services/Roadways/SMS_Roadway_Inventory/FeatureServer/0) adds reported bridge BIN-to-route relationships, roadway/ramp classes and route measures. Its `Lat`/`Long` columns supply reference points, with all 68 original table fields retained. The same Manhattan area returned 15 rows through a bounded live query and complete object-ID fetch. These positions locate table references, not the shape of a ramp or the underside/column of a bridge. See [transport inventories](TRANSPORT-INVENTORIES.md).

The [NYSDOT Height Restricted Bridges service](https://gis.dot.ny.gov/hostingny/rest/services/Geocortex/osowscreen_hoocs/MapServer/2) is now a live bridge-clearance reference source. It retains original bridge points/BINs, carried/crossed road labels, inspection dates and minimum, posted and permitted clearance fields. The [separate scoped live samples](SOURCE-SIZES.md#lower-manhattan-2-km-comparison) verify complete Coney and Manhattan responses. Some fields have zero or high sentinel-like values, and `CONDITION_RATING` is declared as a single-precision float; the adapter checks equivalent native/GeoJSON float32 values while retaining both original representations. Units, codes and crossing direction need field-dictionary validation before physical clearance selection. No point becomes a measured road surface, deck underside or support.

## Overhead-sign assets

The [NYSDOT Overhead Sign Structure layer](https://gis.dot.ny.gov/hostingny/rest/services/Asset/NYSDOT_Structures/FeatureServer/2) is a separate live point source. Acquisition inventories the service `OBJECTID` values, then retains every native and GeoJSON response with `AssetID`, `SIN`, `AssetStatus`, `EditedDate` and `GlobalID`. The [Coney and Manhattan live captures](SOURCE-SIZES.md#lower-manhattan-2-km-comparison) verify complete paired records, including retired assets and a null `AssetID`; source identity therefore uses the validated service `OBJECTID`. State sign points and NYC miscellaneous-structure outlines stay distinct until a real identity match is established. A point does not give sign-face orientation, gantry width/height or individual post locations, so the current renderer keeps it as a reference marker.

OSM's original `min_height` and `building:min_level` tags and NYC building-footprint feature codes 1006 (cantilevered building) and 2110 (skybridge) are retained as potential under-building clues. The [NYC footprint metadata](https://github.com/CityOfNewYork/nyc-geo-metadata/blob/main/Metadata/Metadata_BuildingFootprints.md) names those codes. These tags/classes do not by themselves survey an underside surface or prove a walkable passage. The Manhattan and Coney observations are counted beside their [source-size manifests](SOURCE-SIZES.md#lower-manhattan-2-km-comparison). The observed Luna Park openings have no verified explicit clearance in the current area records; model decisions stay with rendering.

## Original NYC planimetrics

[The original-service registry](../data/nyc-planimetrics.js) adds transport structures, rail structures/lines, curbs, pavement edges, retaining walls, curb cuts, cooling towers and miscellaneous structure polygons. The first group preserves recorded vertex Z; cooling towers and miscellaneous polygons have no Z channel or height fields in the inspected service schemas. Every property, classification, status and source ID remains available. The service's survey edition does not establish a fresh observation date for every carried-forward feature.

Land/context services are also connected: parking lots, plazas, parks, open space outside parks, sidewalk lines, swimming pools, unknown construction areas and cartographic pavement edges. They retain classifications, names, land-use/status fields and original coordinates as references. An outline does not supply a pool's depth, a park's vegetation model or a construction site's replacement building. The cartographic pavement service uses `OBJECTID_1` as its declared object identity; its separate `OBJECTID` and recorded Z fields remain intact. It is another representation of the same survey, not independent confirmation.

Acquisition inventories object IDs, then fetches complete native JSON and geographic GeoJSON in bounded POST batches. Native requests use the layer's declared original coordinate system and retain curves, XYZ/M and response metadata. GeoJSON reprojects horizontal coordinates to EPSG:4326 while leaving the observed Z channel unchanged; native/geographic attribute conflicts reject the snapshot. Missing or unexpected native CRS declarations are rejected. Original responses remain in the source inventory, and normalized references also retain full geographic geometry through `sourceGeometry`.

The [official capture rules](https://github.com/CityOfNewYork/nyc-planimetrics/blob/main/Capture_Rules.md) declare NAVD88 and different capture roles. A rail-station outline can follow a roof, and a curb-cut line describes an opening rather than a complete pedestrian ramp. Horizontal US-foot metadata does not independently prove the exact vertical foot convention. These observations remain references; source merging does not convert vertex Z into a deck grade, wall height, ground sample or equipment solid.

Original ArcGIS layers and Socrata exports can share upstream surveys. They are complementary channels until edition, source identifiers, classification and geometry establish an association. Keeping both is preferable to suppressing uncertain observations, and they never count as independent corroborating surveys merely because they came from different endpoints.

## Pedestrian ramps

[Pedestrian Ramp Locations](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/Transportation/Pedestrian-Ramp-Locations/ufzp-rrqu) adds reported locations, ramp/corner IDs, survey dates, curb reveal, widths, lengths, slopes and condition fields. [Program Progress](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/Transportation/Pedestrian-Ramp-Program-Progress/e7gc-ub6z) adds separate location/work records and construction status/dates. Full response pages, metadata and original numeric strings are retained.

These sources remain separate reference points. Corner IDs relate records without proving one physical ramp, and program status never overwrites a dated measurement. Negative or missing values stay unchanged; units, sentinel meanings and orientation need field-specific validation before rendering. These are pedestrian access records, not highway on-ramp grades or support locations.

## MTA stations

[MTA Subway Stations](https://data.ny.gov/Transportation/MTA-Subway-Stations/39hk-dx4f) is queried from the State Open Data API. Acquisition validates the provider schema, inventories station MRNs inside the GTFS latitude/longitude bounds, then requests every column and the system row ID for exactly those MRNs. Unexpected, duplicate or missing IDs and invalid centroids reject the snapshot. Original responses and full metadata are retained, including fields unused today. This sequence establishes ID coverage, not an atomic provider snapshot across requests.

Normalization uses the documented **GTFS station centroid**. The original `georeference` remains an alternative field; it is not substituted for the GTFS coordinates. Structure type, station/complex MRNs, GTFS stop ID, line, division, routes, direction labels and accessibility are source observations. Structure type does not establish a platform elevation or column location. Station centroids remain references alongside physical station polygons and OSM records; proximity/name alone never suppresses them. See [the source/render boundary](SOURCE-RENDER-BOUNDARY.md).

[MTA Subway Entrances and Exits: 2024](https://data.ny.gov/Transportation/MTA-Subway-Entrances-and-Exits-2024/i9wp-a4ja) adds reported entrance latitude/longitude, type and entry/exit permissions. Full columns and metadata remain available. Distinct entrance system row IDs are the acquisition identities; station IDs are relationships and cannot identify a unique entrance. The shared transit adapter fetches fixed IDs in bounded batches, rejecting missing, repeated or unexpected records. Entrance coordinates never substitute for station centroids, and entrance types do not measure stair geometry, platform height or supports.

## NYC 2017 LiDAR

The public [NOAA distribution](https://noaa-nos-coastal-lidar-pds.s3.amazonaws.com/laz/geoid18/9306/index.html) supplies a browser-accessible [EPT entry point](https://noaa-nos-coastal-lidar-pds.s3.amazonaws.com/entwine/geoid18/9306/ept.json). The adapter traverses intersecting hierarchy pages and acquires original LASzip payloads at **every intersecting depth**. [EPT is additive](https://entwine.io/en/latest/entwine-point-tile.html): omitting ancestors or stopping at a preview level loses points. We neither decimate nor replace coarse nodes with fine nodes.

The capture retains the full EPT schema and CRS, fetched hierarchy responses, complete origin inventory, metadata for original source tiles intersecting the acquisition envelope, all read compressed bytes, LAS headers, SHA-256 checksums and exact requests. `OriginId` still indexes the complete provider origin list. Original tile summaries outside the envelope remain linked in that list; their separate detail JSON is not fetched. No original point attributes are discarded from the compressed payloads.

The delivered CRS is EPSG:6347 (NAD83(2011)/UTM18N), with EPSG:5703 NAVD88 heights in metres. Geographic area selection uses a GRS80 UTM18N envelope with an explicit frame allowance; its padding is defined in [lidar-query.js](../data/lidar-query.js). This is a conservative acquisition selection, **not** a WGS84/NAD83 transformation or a vertical datum alignment. Original XYZ is never shifted. Selected payloads include complete ancestor nodes and tile-edge points outside the requested box; the stored point count describes all fetched payloads, not an exact count inside the box. Renderer point queries can crop explicit native-coordinate boxes while preserving the complete acquired cloud.

**Live point payloads are decoded during acquisition** with the pinned [laz-perf worker](../../../vendor/laz-perf/README.md). The browser queries the public hierarchy and tiles for the selected area, then decodes locally; manual downloads and an external decoding service are unnecessary. Header/count/format/record-length agreement, each decoded coordinate's header envelope and each recorded `OriginId` are checked. Malformed streams reject when the decoder or validation reports a failure; this does not prove that every possible corruption or unused trailing byte is detected.

Each `data.assets[].decoded` retains validation evidence: decoded-record SHA-256, byte/count/format/layout coverage, bounds/class counts, extra-dimension descriptors, decoder provenance and a `payloadId` reference with `encoding: 'laszip'`. The complete lossless records remain in original compressed `raw.payloads`; acquisition releases the redundant decoded buffer after validation. Portable JSON exports include those original tiles and evidence, with no external file/store dependency. LAS/VLR layout determines offsets, not EPT schema order: the captured format-1 records carry `ClassFlags` and `OriginId` extras, while a schema dimension absent from that layout is not invented. Standard return/scan/classification flags, intensity, GPS time, user/source IDs and every extra byte remain accessible. [archivedPointView](../data/lidar-records.js) verifies the compact archive reference, compressed bytes/header and decoded checksum before exposing exact records through [pointRecordView](../data/las-point-records.js). Older decoded-base64 exports remain readable after their recorded decoded byte count and SHA-256 are checked. Both paths apply recorded LAS scale/offset, with no frame/datum shift or reconstruction.

The merge operates on uniquely identified assets and retains complete point archives in `plan.observations.records`. Its log carries validation metadata/checksums instead of repeating large byte strings. The compiler currently does not consume point geometry, so its input carries summaries; restored renderer inventories receive the complete originals. [queryLiDARPoints](../render/lidar-points.js) decodes intersecting tiles sequentially in an owned worker and selects measured records by mandatory native XYZ bounds and optional numeric class/origin filters. `world.queryPoints(sourceId, options)` exposes it for loaded sources, including observations retained behind visibility controls. Results include asset/index identity, original fields/record bytes, source frame, filters and examined/matched counts. Missing validation evidence, archive-integrity failure or result-limit overflow rejects the query without a truncated result or replacement network query; cancellation is checked during decoding and between bounded scan batches. Bounds/filter inputs are captured before asynchronous scanning. Selected byte copies avoid retaining a full tile through a small result. Base64 materialization/checksums still have a per-tile allocation cost; queries run outside the frame loop and do not cache decoded clouds. Classification interpretation, fidelity ranking and surface/support reconstruction remain pending. Tile envelopes are acquisition metadata, never surveyed roofs, decks or pillars. Checkboxes exclude assets from selection while keeping originals in the observation archive. Older captures without validation evidence remain available and show decoding pending; loading them does not silently replace or regenerate their measurements.

Coverage budgets for area size, all vector providers, roof nodes/leaves/features/vertices/bytes and LiDAR hierarchy/tiles/points/compressed/decoded bytes are centralized in [acquisition-limits.js](../data/acquisition-limits.js). Help reads these values directly. API batch sizes and EPT protocol depth constraints remain separate. Full-density acquisition, all additive EPT depths and complete-source rejection remain unchanged. Socrata and MTA inventory pagination request a final empty page to prove completeness at the exact row cap; an extra row is retained as rejection evidence. Errors report requested counts or tile bytes against the configured budget. A shared streaming budget bounds total native geometry/attribute bytes before full response allocation, including concurrent channels; LiDAR's compressed responses use the same bounded reader. Acquisition counts include complete intersecting tiles, which can contain points/buildings outside the exact rectangle. Decoded-byte totals measure exact validated record bytes, not simultaneously retained RAM. A point-count budget alone does not override byte limits. Historical synthetic maximum-allocation checks under `.tmp/map-lab/gathering-review/` used earlier budgets and tested sources separately, not the new maxima or a combined scene.

Areas exceeding a limit reject the source snapshot and retain completed read/decoded evidence separately; choose a smaller area. A rejected or cancelled capture never masquerades as a complete lower-resolution cloud. Large areas can still acquire other sources successfully. Fetch and the 2D preview resolve observations only; render estimates begin at Generate 3D. One owned worker decodes assets sequentially and is terminated on success, failure or cancellation; there are no decoder retries or fallbacks. HTTP sequences are not an immutable provider transaction. The user authorized the decoder dependency exception; no build step, source-service proxy or local-file fallback was added.

JSON downloads stream measured fields through a lab-scoped download worker with backpressure and an attachment response. They do not construct a whole-archive string or Blob. [parseJSONStream](../data/json-reader.js) reads UTF-8 chunks from a response body or Node readable stream, so exported archives larger than V8's single-string limit remain consumable. It preserves standard JSON fields, duplicate-key semantics and prototype-named data properties; malformed/incomplete input or cancellation yields no partial snapshot. Live queries still supply source objects directly, without a local-file fallback. The worker handles only one-use download URLs, rejects unknown tokens and other methods, and releases unclaimed streams; it does not cache or intercept source API/game requests. Keep the page open while its in-memory archive is exporting. This transport requires a secure browser context (HTTPS or localhost).

## Pier drawings

NYSDOT pier drawings remain public online documents with edition/URL provenance; see [support sources](SUPPORT-SOURCES.md). They supply standard design references, not a geographic feature API. An actual GET of the current PDF returned PDF bytes, but advertised no browser CORS permission. The PDF has not become a live spatial source or an estimated support layer. Project-specific column/cap placement remains unavailable.

## Verification and retained results

`tests/native-layers-test.mjs` verifies actual MTA fields/centroids, original point bytes/checksums, additive hierarchy, rejection evidence, source-only merge and selection. `tests/native-layers-browser-test.mjs` checks point-only input, source downloads, worker restoration, reference stations and Help. The attributed fixture contains complete scoped MTA records and two original LiDAR binary samples; its hierarchy is constructed for deterministic traversal tests, not presented as a complete live cloud. [Testing](TESTING.md) lists commands.

Live captures are ignored local artifacts under `.tmp/map-lab/native-layers/`; the later `v2/` edition adds original source-tile metadata without overwriting the earlier acquisition. Each edition has individual source JSON and a `summary.json` with bounds, checksums, counts, timestamps and merge retention. Browser CORS results and scoped test reports are retained alongside them. Historical crash captures remain unchanged, and this source acquisition work does not diagnose those crashes.

The original NYC/entrance/pedestrian follow-up is retained separately under `.tmp/map-lab/open-review/`: complete area captures in `captures/`, a checksummed attributed fixture in `tests/fixtures/planimetric-manifest.json`, and scoped model/browser reports. `planimetric-sources` and `planimetric-sources-browser` check native CRS retention/rejection, geographic XYZ, distinct entrances, bounded transit batches, all source-only roles, downloads, worker restoration and Help. The fixture uses selected unmodified records with constructed test inventories; complete live responses remain local evidence. `crash-original/report.json` records the separate natural-collection repeated-generation experiment, not a crash diagnosis.

Worker compilation receives the data/metadata it uses; complete raw archives remain on the original snapshots and are attached to the restored renderer observation inventory. The [compiler input contract](BENCHMARK-ARCHITECTURE.md) avoids a duplicate archive copy while preserving every original field and byte for later interpretation. This independent performance change does not resolve a historical crash.

The land/context follow-up retains complete bounded service responses and checksums under `.tmp/map-lab/close-findings/captures/`. Its separate `land-context-manifest.json` fixture records selected unmodified native/geographic records, full metadata, dates and attribution. The shared planimetric model/browser checks include these services, their actual object-ID field, visibility, worker restoration, downloads and Help. `live-browser.json` in that local evidence directory records direct browser acquisition separately from recorded-response tests. Earlier fixtures and captures remain unchanged.

The earlier offline/browser experiments retain their original scope under `.tmp/map-lab/close-findings/decoder-probe.json` and `.tmp/map-lab/fix-all/decoder-browser-probe.json`; they preceded runtime integration. The user subsequently authorized pinned laz-perf. `lidar-decoder` checks the vendored binary/license, complete records, actual descriptor offsets, coordinates/origins/classes, byte limits and damaged-stream rejection. `native-layers` and `native-layers-browser` verify acquisition, worker decoding, source-only merging, complete restored inventories/downloads and cancellation. These checks supply point measurements, not missing columns or reconstructed surfaces, and do not diagnose historical browser crashes.
