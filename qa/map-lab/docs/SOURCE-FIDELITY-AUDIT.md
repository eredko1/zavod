# Source fidelity audit and merge plan

Research snapshot: September 27, 2026 (New York time; some acquisition timestamps are September 28 UTC). This covers every connected Map Lab dataset, the NYC planimetric layers omitted from our registry, older editions, and the public sources behind or related to Esri's 3D maps. It records what we can recover from existing data before adding more sources.

The later [transport inventory integration](TRANSPORT-INVENTORIES.md) connects public NYSDOT ramp, roadway, bridge, height-restricted-bridge, overhead-sign and bridge-to-roadway table sources plus the federal 2023 NBI bridge layer. It retains complete source responses and all properties as references, independently of the archived comparison measurements below. Ramp/roadway Z channels in the inspected areas were zero, bridge/clearance points are inventory locations rather than individual supports or underside surfaces, and sign-asset points do not locate their posts. The [building-grade source](SOURCE-ACQUISITION.md#building-grade-and-floor-observations) and [Manhattan source measurements](SOURCE-SIZES.md#lower-manhattan-2-km-comparison) extend this audit without replacing its original samples.

**Recommendation:** keep all source observations and select geometry per physical feature. NYC's older building meshes can improve stepped roof geometry; current OSM parts can be stronger for other buildings. Native meshes and measured rooftop tank envelopes are connected; original NYC vertex elevations, curb cuts and land/context boundaries are now acquired as references for later renderer interpretation. No single dataset wins everywhere. Age alone does not disqualify the 2014 model or the 2023 Overture archive.

The research comparisons below remain an archived observation, distinct from subsequent rendering selection. NYC's 2014 native building meshes and 2022 water tanks are connected to live fetching, merge selection and rendering. Global bridge-level/ramp rules and mapped support classification are implemented too; see [current fidelity integration](FIDELITY-INTEGRATION.md). [Source acquisition](SOURCE-ACQUISITION.md) now also connects original NYC planimetric vertex/curve channels, curb cuts/cooling/miscellaneous outlines, pedestrian-ramp survey/work records, MTA stations/entrances and NOAA LiDAR bytes. These later vectors remain references, and other candidates remain pending. Source merge retains actual measurements and all unique alternatives; estimates and model construction live exclusively in rendering, with separate logs. See [the source/render contract](SOURCE-RENDER-BOUNDARY.md). Current source policy lives in [map-merge-rules.js](../pipeline/map-merge-rules.js), [layering rules](DATA-LAYERING.md) and [the pipeline guide](OSM-PIPELINE.md).

### Recover original Z before inventing heights

Actual queries of NYC's original services confirmed `hasZ:true` and returned coordinate triples for transport structures, railroad structures, railroad lines, curbs, pavement edges, retaining walls and elevation points. Retaining-wall metadata was checked; neither small comparison box contains a wall sample. The wider Coney capture contains walls, but their original Z profiles were not sampled in this pass.

Examples: [Transport Structure 2022](https://services6.arcgis.com/yG5s3afENB5iO9fj/arcgis/rest/services/Transport_Structure_2022/FeatureServer/25), [Railroad Structure 2022](https://services6.arcgis.com/yG5s3afENB5iO9fj/arcgis/rest/services/Railroad_Structure_2022/FeatureServer/18), [Railroad 2022](https://services6.arcgis.com/yG5s3afENB5iO9fj/arcgis/rest/services/Railroad_2022/FeatureServer/17), [Curb 2022](https://services6.arcgis.com/yG5s3afENB5iO9fj/arcgis/rest/services/Curb_2022/FeatureServer/4) and [Pavement Edge 2022](https://services6.arcgis.com/yG5s3afENB5iO9fj/arcgis/rest/services/Pavement_Edge_2022/FeatureServer/14). Layer IDs come from service metadata; do not assume `/0` is the canonical advertised ID.

The Socrata geometries inspected contain XY, and local display projection uses longitude/latitude. Original transport/rail/curb/pavement/retaining-wall channels are now collected separately: native JSON keeps original source coordinates/curves, and normalization retains geographic XYZ through `sourceGeometry`. Requesting `outSR=4326` reprojects XY but leaves the observed Z values unchanged. Elevation point Z matched the numeric `ELEVATION` field in the archived sample. The declared horizontal US-survey-foot coordinates do not independently establish the vertical foot convention; that convention and physical vertex roles remain items to verify before renderer use.

**Matching method:** rasterize the projected union of nondegenerate mesh triangles and polygon interiors, retaining holes. Calculate IoU, intersection fraction of each object, occupied area/area ratio, centroid distance and approximate four-direction Crofton perimeter. Match a unique valid BIN with IoU at least 0.5, or mutually unique geometry with IoU at least 0.8 when valid BINs do not conflict. Review overlap starts at 0.2, following the current policy. The passing pair sets were unchanged at 1 m sampling. Median sampled IoU, area ratio and perimeter ratio were 1, with zero sampled centroid displacement in both areas: many old outlines are unchanged. Individual differences remain in the measurement record; this does not certify all matches or exact boundaries.

These are **candidate identity matches**, not approved geometry replacements. Mesh counts use extent intersection; NYC and OSM query inclusion differs. Absence from one query is not evidence of demolition. The post-2014 construction records are temporal review flags, not a conclusive demolition/rebuild list. A construction year or edited timestamp alone cannot establish exactly which surfaces changed.

Upper horizontal bands were calculated from triangle orientation, excluding the lowest 0.5 m, clustering elevations within 0.25 m and retaining bands with at least 1 m² of projected area. They show stepped surface detail, not a semantic roof count. Total triangles or “upper 20% of height” would incorrectly reward walls/subdivision. Strict edge pairing uses 1 mm vertex quantization; failures may include T-junctions or coincident component faces, so this diagnostic alone does not prove holes. Solid/collision validity needs further topology checks.

### IDs, native access and vertical limits

The earlier SDK query's null BIN/DOITT attributes were incomplete attribute retrieval. Native node attributes contain usable IDs. Conservative hierarchy traversal and decoding of all intersecting leaf nodes reproduced the earlier object sets. Per-feature face ranges, binary lengths, attribute counts and geometry/object IDs were validated. The layer exposes an uncompressed I3S layout alongside compressed representations; the connected bounded `fetch`/`ArrayBuffer` adapter uses it without adding the ArcGIS SDK to Map Lab. Original service responses inspected allowed CORS.

[Esri documents that SceneLayerView queries see loaded visible features and can omit objects at lower detail](https://developers.arcgis.com/javascript/latest/references/core/views/layers/SceneLayerView/). Do not make a production inventory depend on a camera's loaded view, or assume a SceneServer supplies an ordinary FeatureServer `/query` endpoint.

The hosted NYC [scene layer metadata](https://tiles.arcgis.com/tiles/yG5s3afENB5iO9fj/arcgis/rest/services/3D_Buildings_Citywide/SceneServer/layers/0) declares geographic XY and **EGM96 metres** for Z. This differs from the current NYC NAVD88 sources. The table compares relative geometric spans only; it does **not** subtract EGM96 absolute roof elevations from NAVD88 ground/top elevations. Even spans have different meanings when a mesh includes lower components or a sloping base. A verified datum transform or explicitly labelled rebasing policy is required before merged placement.

### Overture height semantics need verification

The [current building-part schema](https://docs.overturemaps.org/schema/reference/buildings/building_part/) describes `height` as lowest-to-highest distance, whereas [OSM defines height as ground-to-top even with min_height](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Simple_3D_Buildings). Actual same-version cross-checks found Overture values copied from OSM: `way/370587019@3` has height `75` and min-height `68` in both. OSM places that part between those elevations relative to ground; adding them would raise its top incorrectly. Probes are preserved in the measurements. Do not implement a blanket `top=min_height+height` conversion from the schema alone. Retain a semantics conflict; use verified original-source meaning where available, and investigate other source families separately.

Current Overture's whole-building samples include Microsoft and USGS height lineage alongside OSM footprints. Those may enrich dimensions, but do not provide independent OSM parts or additional measured roof surfaces. Preserve property-level lineage rather than treating “OSM + Overture + Esri Open 3D” as three agreeing surveys.

## Pillars, supports and elevated structures

The later [support-source review](SUPPORT-SOURCES.md) retains public NYSDOT pier/cap/footing detail drawings; [source acquisition](SOURCE-ACQUISITION.md) now collects actual MTA records and original NOAA LiDAR point bytes. These add design references, transit observations and point evidence without establishing a citywide measured column inventory. Complete captures and checksums are retained separately from this archived audit.

We already have useful deck and rail coverage; most missing volume is a geometry/vertical-data issue rather than a complete absence of elevated infrastructure. Original XYZ improves the evidence for deck edges, tracks and canopy surfaces.

No dedicated measured Coney column dataset was established by this audit. The wide local OSM capture contains no `bridge:support` records. NYC transport/station layers inspected describe outlines/profiles; miscellaneous structures did not provide bridge supports. These are scoped findings, not proof that no public plans or support records exist anywhere.

[OSM bridge-support mapping](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Tag%3Abridge%3Asupport%3Dpier) can supply actual support positions/outlines where mapped; a waterfront `man_made=pier` describes a different object. Dedicated support classification is now implemented. Eligible mapped pylons with tagged heights and one containing road structure use estimated open frames around mapped road corridors. Ground base/member geometry remains explicitly estimated; no nearest-structure attachment or invented regular pillar spacing is used. See [current support limits](FIDELITY-INTEGRATION.md#mapped-supports).

When supports are absent, a procedural model is possible only as a separate estimated render candidate. Spacing, section, abutments and deck underside/thickness would be documented assumptions. Exclude road/track clearances using validated geometry; do not present evenly spaced invented pillars as surveyed objects. Aerial LiDAR may help exposed surfaces but cannot reliably see hidden columns/undersides. Project drawings or suitable ground observations would provide stronger support geometry; none were obtained here.

## How the expanded merge should work

1. **Preserve first.** Store raw source bodies/binaries, complete attributes, bounds, requests, acquisition time, imagery/survey time, release/version, licence/attribution, checksums, coordinate/vertical reference and source lineage. Retain duplicate, rejected and unmatched observations. Rendering selection changes a decision, not the original record.
2. **Classify physical roles.** Separate whole buildings, parts, roof equipment, decks, tracks, platforms, canopies, supports, ground surfaces and network records. A tank is not another building; a roof is not a platform; a graph line is not pavement. Map source-specific field names/units explicitly.
3. **Establish identity.** Prefer validated BIN/DOITT/GlobalID or upstream crosswalks, always with geometry sanity and uniqueness. Include feature type, edition and upstream version in identity. Preserve one-to-many/split/ambiguous candidates. GERS is useful for buildings; building-part feature IDs are not automatically stable GERS crosswalks. See [Overture bridge-file limits](https://docs.overturemaps.org/gers/bridge-files/).
4. **Validate eligibility before fidelity.** Resolve height meaning, units/datum, completeness, physical level and temporal conflicts first. Unknowns remain explicit. Older compatible geometry stays eligible; positive change evidence can disqualify it for current rendering without deleting it.
5. **Compare usable geometry.** Assess mutual footprint coverage, displacement, area/perimeter differences, courtyard/part coverage, roof steps/slopes, surface/topology validity, registered vertical consistency and measured-versus-estimated dimensions. Triangle density is not fidelity. Rank separate geometry aspects with recorded evidence; do not invent one universal weighted score before calibration.
6. **Select measured geometry; model separately.** Source merge chooses supported whole/part/roof observations while retaining every complete alternative. Rendering selects assembled volumes and attaches equipment after roof/support checks. It must assess part completeness and avoid duplicate complete solids or incompatible roof splices; model estimates never feed back as source measurements.
7. **Keep decisions reviewable.** Extend existing merge members, selected-field provenance, reasons and rule version with candidate metrics and statuses such as selected, represented, unmatched, temporal-review, ambiguous, semantics-conflict and datum-unresolved. Separate 2D reference visibility from 3D geometry selection. Source toggles must re-resolve enabled alternatives; disabling a source cannot let it suppress another source.
8. **Preserve partial coverage and cost evidence.** Merge compatible road/curb intervals and surfaces without erasing uncovered segments, holes, graph connectivity or stacked levels. Keep mesh sampling/LOD and collision simplification separate from source selection. Measure generation/buffer and frame costs on the same saved scene; exporting originals must not force every alternative into GPU memory.

Likely combinations: current NYC footprint/identity plus a compatible 2014 stepped mesh; current OSM parts plus verified NYC roof equipment; original NYC deck/rail Z plus OSM connectivity; NYC sidewalk/curb alignment plus measured cuts/access tags. Each combination needs its own role and geometry gate, not proximity-only fusion.

## Implementation order and remaining checks

### Use the existing live area-fetch flow

The NYC 2014 mesh and 2022 water tanks now participate in the existing **select area → Fetch area → inspect sources → resolve merge → Generate 3D** flow. Their adapters receive the same bounds, cancellation and source progress reporting. Mesh acquisition traverses intersecting native nodes, validates complete geometry/identity attributes and retains original bytes. Water tanks use complete object-ID batches and preserve native and GeoJSON responses. Camera-loaded features are not used as an area inventory.

Fetched node metadata, raw mesh/attribute buffers and decoded records remain in snapshots/exports with requests, bounds and timestamps. New captures of the other NYC sources retain full response pages and full metadata too. The same snapshots drive preview, toggles and generation. Normal NYC Fetch area includes both new layers; source checkboxes select rendering inputs. Acquisition limits can reject an oversized/incomplete source while other successful sources remain available. Research fixtures are test evidence and never a production fallback; no citywide download or separate cache framework is required.

“Live” describes fetching the hosted service on demand. It does not change the model's 2014 capture date. Existing identity, temporal, datum and geometry gates still determine whether each fetched mesh can supply rendered geometry.

### Geometry and adapter sequence

1. Recover original NYC Z with complete ID-batched acquisition, subtype metadata and explicit units/vertical reference. Validate profile semantics against spot observations before activating placement.
2. Water tanks are connected with parent/roof/base validation and measured envelope rendering. Curb cuts, cooling towers and miscellaneous outlines are now acquired as references; modeling them remains deferred. Cooling towers need supported height before a solid. Preserve parent/child provenance.
3. Native NYC mesh selection is connected with identity/detail/height/date gates and explicitly estimated base rebasing. Raw Overture adapters still need lineage/height-semantics gates; do not silently substitute incompatible height meanings or datums.
4. Construct OSM roof forms and resolve incomplete/ambiguous part assemblies. The renderer now checks exact complete footprint coverage before parent replacement; this does not prove surveyed volume completeness. Mapped support roles and eligible open pylon estimates are implemented; unknown supports and structural member details remain gaps. Bring in remaining surface/context layers where they fill verified gaps.
5. Process a bounded LiDAR tile sample to test useful roof/deck/structure returns. Point-cloud reconstruction is additional work; catalogue availability does not establish geometric improvement.

Adapter tests must cover immutable raw records, ID reuse/placeholders, complete batching/hierarchy traversal, ambiguity and changed footprints, part coverage, height semantics and datums, clipping/caps, duplicate suppression, source toggles and export restoration. Geometry checks must distinguish rendering from valid solids/colliders. Browser/performance work uses the repository's Xvfb launcher and actual WebGL renderer/timer verification.

Archived research validation: existing `map-merge`, `osm-model`, `merge-selection`, `nyc-selection` and `browser-display` checks passed. Native binary/attribute consistency and two-grid candidate matching were separately checked. The actual ArcGIS viewer and Overture exporter ran under Xvfb with hardware WebGL. Later implementation checks and their actual scope are documented in [fidelity integration](FIDELITY-INTEGRATION.md) and [testing](TESTING.md). Neither pass certifies LiDAR reconstruction or surveyed pillar/member accuracy.
